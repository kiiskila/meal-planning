Assumptions and flags

What changed in this revision, and the defaults I chose. Tell me if any of them is wrong.

  • She is vegetarian. All 20 shared recipes are vegetarian (eggs and dairy are used; tell me if she avoids either). I read each ingredient list for meat, poultry, stock and seafood-derived sauces. Soups and curry are made with vegetable broth.
  • His meat is separate. One small batch a week, cooked on Sunday only for him: chicken teriyaki, ginger pork or kotlety mielone. He gets a portion on each workday lunch, on Wednesday's dinner and on a weekend lunch; the other meals are vegetarian for both of you. Leave it out and his day drops by about 160 to 220 kcal and 14 to 24 g protein.
  • Two people. Both of you eat all four meals from the plan every day, including a packed lunch each on workdays. Cooked dishes are split 4 parts his to 3 parts hers; sides are listed separately for each of you.
  • His target: lose weight slowly. The menus give him 2025 to 2326 kcal a day (average 2174), roughly 450 to 500 below my estimate of his maintenance (about 2,700 with strength training and an active commute), or a little under half a kilo a week. Protein averages 125 g a day (range 98 to 152), lower than the first plan because most meals are meatless. If strength drops, add Greek yogurt or a boiled egg before adding starch.
  • Her target: maintain. I do not know her weight, activity or goal, so I assumed maintenance for a moderately active 5'6" woman: 1671 to 1882 kcal a day (average 1737) and about 90 g protein, from tofu, eggs, yogurt, cottage cheese, lentils and beans.
  • No seafood. No fish or shellfish, and no fish sauce, oyster sauce, bonito dashi, anchovy or Worcestershire in any recipe.
  • No raw tomatoes for him. Nothing in this version uses raw tomato. The egg muffins bake cherry tomatoes in, and the curry and chili cook tomato into the sauce.
  • Cuisine mix. 10 Japanese, Korean or Asian-style recipes and 6 Polish or Polish-style ones, plus chili, lentil soup, breakfasts and snacks.
  • Budget. Costs are Save-On-Foods Pandora prices seen on November 10, 2026 for the amount each recipe uses. Tofu, eggs, potatoes, beans and lentils keep it low.
  • Options, not weeks. Pick any option for the season. Each covers all seven days: packed lunches Monday to Friday, and a Saturday cook that also feeds Sunday.
  • Weekends. Breakfast is eggs on toast with yogurt or fruit (not a linked recipe, just sides). Saturday has two cooks, a lunch dish and a dinner dish, each made big enough for Sunday too. The last of his meat goes on his weekend lunch. Soup and chili weekends put two extra servings in the freezer.
  • Nutrition numbers. Recipe figures are the per-serving values on each recipe page. Sides use rounded reference values (my estimates).
  • Could not verify. H Mart shelf prices (flyer only): gochujang, soba and sake carry no cost line and sit inside a $6 per option allowance with spices, flour and oil. Costco, farmers market and butcher prices. Most of the Thrifty Foods flyer. Whether the cheeses are made with animal rennet. Canadian Tire showed prices for its default store, not a Victoria one.

How an option works

The same rhythm every time, so the two of you only have to agree on which option.

Sunday, about 2 hoursCook the Monday to Wednesday lunch, the breakfast, the snack, and his small batch of meat. Soup and chili options also cook the Thursday and Friday lunch now and freeze it.
Monday eveningCook dinner one, vegetarian for both. It covers tonight and tomorrow.
Wednesday eveningThe Thursday and Friday lunch dish is tonight's dinner, usually a 25 to 40 minute cook. His meat goes on his plate.
Thursday eveningCook dinner two for tonight and Friday.
SaturdayTwo relaxed cooks: a lunch dish and a dinner dish, each sized to cover Sunday as well. Sunday is leftovers plus next week's prep.
SplittingLine up his and her containers and fill them 4 parts to 3. A kitchen scale makes it exact: his box gets 57%% of each pair, hers 43%%.

Buy in bulk, then freeze

  • Oats, brown rice, lentils and quinoa from bulk bins (25% off at Fairway this week) or large bags.
  • Pork loin and chicken thigh value packs: split into one-recipe bags and freeze.
  • Freezes well: vegetarian chili, lentil soup, borscht, Japanese curry (without the potatoes), uncooked kopytka, kotlety, ginger pork, teriyaki chicken, egg muffins, baked oatmeal, energy balls, granola bars, sliced bread.
  • Do not freeze: tofu dishes (the texture changes), mizeria, soba salad, potato salad, bibimbap vegetables.

  • Buy tofu, ginger, green onions and mushrooms at H Mart on the way past.
